# Attorney Website Advertising Rules Notes

Every US state treats a law firm website as attorney advertising in some form. The details differ, but the same handful of topics come up in almost every state's rules. These notes help a firm know what to check before publishing. They are general information; always read your own state's current rules or ask your bar's ethics hotline. Published by Legal Web Agency.

## Topics to check in your state's rules

| Topic | Typical question |
|-------|------------------|
| Required disclaimers | Do we need "attorney advertising" or a past-results disclaimer? |
| Case results | Can we show verdicts and settlements, and with what context? |
| Testimonials and reviews | Are client quotes allowed, and do they need a disclaimer? |
| "Specialist" and "expert" | Can we use these words without certification? |
| Comparisons | Are words like "best" or "top" allowed? |
| Responsible attorney | Must an attorney's name and office address appear? |
| Record keeping | Do we need to keep copies of the site or ads? |

## A simple review routine

1. Keep a list of every page that mentions results, reviews or credentials.
2. Review those pages whenever the rules or the page change.
3. Date-stamp changes so you can show what was published and when.
